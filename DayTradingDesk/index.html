Module 1 · Foundations

Day trading is risk management with a chart attached

A day trader opens and closes every position in the same session, so nothing is held overnight. You make money by capturing a slice of the day's movement more often, or by a bigger amount, than the market takes from you. The edge is small, so costs, position size and discipline decide the outcome.

Read context

Before the open: overnight high/low, prior day high/low/close, economic calendar (8:30 ET data, 10:00 ET data, FOMC at 2:00 ET). Decide whether today looks like a trend day or a range day.

Wait for a setup

A setup is a pre-defined pattern with a reason to exist: a break of the opening range, a pullback to VWAP, a failed breakout. No setup, no trade.

Size from the stop

Place the stop where the idea is proven wrong, then pick the contract count so that stop costs ≤ 1% of your account. Size is the output, never the input.

Execute with a bracket

Enter with a stop-loss and profit target attached (an OCO bracket). The platform manages the exit, so you don't negotiate with a losing trade.

Journal and review

Log every trade with the setup name and a grade. After 50–100 trades, the numbers show which setups actually pay you.

Market mechanics

The order book and the spread

Buyers rest bids below the market, sellers rest offers above it. A market order takes the best price on the other side immediately. A limit order waits at your price. A stop order becomes a market order once price touches it. NQ normally trades one tick (0.25 pts) wide.

Sample depth-of-market around 24,500.00. Green = resting bids (buyers), red = resting offers (sellers).

Leverage, in one line

One MNQ at 24,500 controls $49,000

Notional = price × multiplier = 24,500 × $2. A normal 250-point NQ day moves one micro contract $500. The same move on one full NQ is $5,000. Leverage cuts both ways at exactly the same speed.

The math that matters

Expectancy = (W × avg win) − (L × avg loss)

A 40% win rate is profitable if winners average 2R. A 70% win rate loses money if losers are 3× the winners. See the simulator under Risk tools.

Base rates

Most retail day traders lose money

Academic studies of retail day traders consistently find that a large majority lose money over time and only a small minority are persistently profitable. Treat the first months as paid education: simulator first, then 1 micro contract.

The E-mini Nasdaq-100 (NQ) and Micro E-mini Nasdaq-100 (MNQ) track the same index and move point-for-point. The micro is one tenth the size, which makes it the right contract to learn on.

Contract months & roll

Which contract to trade today

Equity index futures expire quarterly on the third Friday of March (H), June (M), September (U) and December (Z). Volume moves to the next contract about 8 days before expiry. That's the roll. Always trade the contract with the most volume.

What moves NQ

Scheduled catalysts

  • 8:30 ET CPI, PPI, jobs report (1st Friday), jobless claims (Thu), retail sales
  • 10:00 ET ISM, consumer sentiment, JOLTS
  • 2:00 ET FOMC rate decision (8×/year), press conference 2:30
  • After 4:00 ET Mega-cap earnings: NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META, TSLA
Character

How NQ behaves

  • More volatile than ES (S&P 500). Moves of 1–2% a day are common.
  • Top ~10 tech names are roughly half the index weight.
  • Opening 30–60 min carries the most range and volume.
  • Round numbers (24,500, 25,000) and prior-day levels act as magnets.
Alternatives

Other liquid day-trading futures

  • MES / ES S&P 500: smoother, slower; good second market.
  • MYM / YM Dow: $0.50 / $5 per point.
  • M2K / RTY Russell 2000: choppier, small caps.
  • MCL / CL Crude oil: news-driven, larger gaps. Not for beginners.
