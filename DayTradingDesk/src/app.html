          Foundations

          How day trading NQ futures works

          A day trader opens and closes every position within one session, so nothing is held overnight. The edge is small, so costs, position size and discipline decide whether you keep any of it.

          Start here · the whole idea in 60 seconds

          1 · What you trade

          MNQ is a contract that tracks the Nasdaq-100. Every 1-point move is $2 per contract. NQ moves about 200 points on a normal day, so one MNQ swings about $400.

          2 · How you win

          Wait for a tested setup, enter with a stop (where you're wrong) already placed, and let winners run further than losers. You can win only 39% of trades and still make money if wins are 2× losses.

          3 · How you pass an eval

          Make the profit target without your balance ever touching the max loss limit, which follows your best end-of-day balance up. Small size and patience beat big size and hope.

          Reading a candlestick

          Each candle is one time block, 5 minutes on this site's charts. Green means price closed higher than it opened; red means lower. The thin wicks show how far price stretched before pulling back.

          Visual guide

          Points, ticks and dollars

          1 point = 4 ticks. The smallest move is one tick (0.25 pts), worth $0.50 on MNQ and $5 on NQ. The bar compares your move with a typical NQ day.

          Visual guide

          A bracket order: your trade's seatbelt

          A bracket places your stop-loss and profit target together with the entry. Whichever gets hit first closes the trade and cancels the other (that's "OCO", one-cancels-other). You decide the risk before emotions kick in.

          Eval survival

          Six ways beginners fail evals, and the fix for each

          Oversizing

          Trading 10–20 micros because the platform allows 40. One normal 50-point pullback on 20 MNQ is −$2,000: the whole account.

          Fix: size so a full stop costs ≤ 10% of the max loss.

          Revenge trading

          Taking a new trade right after a loss to "win it back", usually bigger and without a setup.

          Fix: 2 losses in a row = done for the day. The Today tab's gate enforces it.

          Forgetting the intraday check

          "End-of-day drawdown" sounds like only the close counts. It doesn't. Dipping to the limit at 11 am ends the account even if you'd have recovered.

          Fix: know your MLL number every morning. Your stop must sit above it.

          One lucky day

          A +$1,800 day on a $3,000 target breaks the 50% consistency rule. You'd need $3,600 total to pass.

          Fix: cap each day around 30–40% of the target. Use the checker above.

          Trading the news

          CPI and FOMC can move NQ 100+ points in seconds, and stops can fill far past their price (slippage).

          Fix: be flat before scheduled releases.

          Moving the stop

          Dragging a stop further away "to give it room" turns a planned −$60 loss into −$600.

          Fix: brackets on every trade. Stops only move toward profit, never away.

          Market mechanics

          The order book and the spread

          Bids rest below the market and offers rest above it. A market order fills immediately against the other side. A limit order waits at your price. A stop order turns into a market order when price touches it. NQ is usually one tick (0.25 pts) wide.
